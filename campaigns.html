Campaigns

Fourteen campaigns, defined and drawn

A campaign is the level of planning that sits between the Party’s strategic guidance and the individual battle. The PLA distinguishes joint campaigns, which involve several services, from the campaigns of a single service, and its texts name a fairly standard set of types. This page defines fourteen of them, with an original schematic for each that shows the logic of the operation and not a particular plan.

What to take from this page

01

Campaigns link guidance to battle

The PLA separates joint campaigns from service campaigns, and the CNA paper suggests its 1999 joint guidelines named joint blockade, joint landing and joint air defence campaigns. The 2020 guidelines are meant to standardise the most important types.

02

Blockade and landing are two ends of one logic

The strategy text names island blockade and island landing as the two main kinds of island operation. A blockade can isolate a target, prepare a landing or serve as a coercive option on its own.

03

Offence is the larger half of the list, defence the hinge

Most entries are offensive, yet the strategic aim is defensive. Anti-air raid, positional defence, sea-lane protection and naval base defence are where active defence meets the order of battle.

04

Terrain defines the land campaigns

Mountain offensive and positional defence are the entries closest to the Himalayan frontier. The strategy text’s border defence section, with its defence, counterattack and timely withdrawal, is the nearest match to that case.

05

Maritime protection is growing in importance

The text expects escort and sea-lane protection to widen beyond anti-piracy, and to become more joint and more cooperative with foreign forces.

06

Detail in the sources is uneven

Each entry carries a basis tag. Entries tagged general logic describe the type of campaign in outline because the sources give little detail, and they should be read as such.
